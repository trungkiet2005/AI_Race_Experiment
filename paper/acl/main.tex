\begin{abstract}
Studies that read language-model behaviour as strategy increasingly admit
models to analysis through a comprehension screen: a battery of questions
about the task, scored against ground truth, that a model must pass before
its choices are interpreted. A screen is a measurement instrument and a
selection rule at once, so its validity is the validity of every behavioural
claim downstream of it, and it is almost never treated as an instrument. We
ask what such a screen measures. Using a frozen battery of twenty probes over
six comprehension domains, administered three times each to nine commercial
endpoints on a repeated economic game, we report three findings. Comprehension
fractionates: reading the stage payoff matrix is solved perfectly by every
endpoint, rebuilding the current state of a game in progress separates them
across almost the whole range, and no endpoint computes a closed-form expected
payoff, so an aggregate screen score averages a subtask that separates nothing
with the only subtask that separates anything. Output-format compliance
discriminates nothing, and in one battery it runs opposite to accuracy: the
domain every answer got right is the domain whose requested format was never
once produced. And the verdict is cheap to reproduce. One probe asked three
times reproduces the battery's admit-or-refuse split on all nine endpoints, as
does a regular expression over the endpoint name. We set out the criteria a
screen must meet before its verdict may select an analysis sample, place
discriminant validity at their centre, and name the evidence this battery
still needs to meet it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% EVIDENCE  The problem, the gap, the contributions.
% ARTIFACT  docs/novelty-survey-2026-09-10.md holds candidate leads for the
%           citations this section needs. STATUS for citations: NEEDS
%           ANALYSIS. Every one must be verified at the source before use.
% =====================================================================

A behavioural study of language-model agents has to decide which models it is
about. The decision is increasingly made by a comprehension screen: before any
choice is interpreted as strategy, the model answers a fixed set of questions
about the task, its answers are scored against ground truth, and only models
above a threshold enter the analysis. The reasoning is sound. An action that
parses is not evidence that the agent followed the game, and a study that reads
arbitrary output as policy measures its own prompt.

A screen is therefore two things at once, and only one of them gets checked. It
is a measurement instrument, and it is a selection rule on the sample every
later claim is made about. Whatever the screen fails to measure, the study
inherits; whatever the screen is accidentally correlated with, the study's
sample is selected on. Nobody reports the screen's reliability, nobody reports
what its verdict would have been at a different threshold, and nobody reports
what the screen buys over signals that cost nothing.

This paper treats an admission screen as an instrument and puts it through the
tests an instrument gets. We ask whether the battery measures one thing or
several, whether the same battery administered twice returns the same verdict,
whether the verdict survives a band of thresholds, whether the verdict is
reproduced by a cheaper signal, and whether the score predicts anything about
the behaviour it gates. The fourth of those is the question the paper exists to
ask, because it is the one a screen can fail while passing all the others.

Our contributions are the following.

\begin{itemize}
\item A set of criteria an admission screen must meet before its verdict may be
      used to select an analysis sample (\S\ref{sec:construct}), with
      discriminant validity against cheap signals as the operative one.
\item Evidence that comprehension fractionates across subtasks sharply enough
      that an aggregate screen score is carried by one domain
      (\S\ref{sec:res-fractionate}), and that output-format compliance carries
      no information about it (\S\ref{sec:res-format}).
\item A reliability and threshold analysis of one screen
      (\S\ref{sec:res-stability}), including two admitted endpoints that clear
      a condition by a margin of zero, on an instrument that moves between
      identical administrations.
\item A discriminant-validity comparison against four cheaper screens
      (\S\ref{sec:res-name}). One probe asked three times, and a regular
      expression over the endpoint name, each reproduce the sixty-answer
      verdict exactly.
\item A statement of what a screen must be shown before it earns its cost, and
      an account of what this battery still lacks (\S\ref{sec:families},
      \S\ref{sec:res-transfer}).
\end{itemize}

The scope is one battery, one game and nine commercial endpoints, which is why
the paper's claim is about what a screen must be shown rather than about
screens in general. \S\ref{sec:boundary} states the relation to a concurrent
submission by the same authors that uses this screen as an entry condition.

\section{What a Screen Must Do}
\label{sec:construct}
% EVIDENCE  The paper's conceptual core. Argument, not measurement.
% ARTIFACT  NONE.
% STATUS    HAVE (writable now).
% =====================================================================

A screen earns its place only against a standard, so we state one. The six
criteria below are what an admission battery must be shown to satisfy before
its verdict may decide which models a behavioural study is about. They are not
novel statistics; they are the ordinary requirements on a measuring instrument,
applied to an object the field currently treats as a configuration setting.

\paragraph{C1, construct coverage.} The battery's domains span the abilities
the downstream task actually requires. A screen that tests recall of stated
rules does not license reading trajectories as strategy, because playing a
repeated game requires carrying a state forward, not repeating a sentence.

\paragraph{C2, measured fractionation.} Per-domain scores are reported, not an
aggregate alone. An aggregate can be carried entirely by one domain while
another contributes no variance, and a reader who sees only the aggregate
cannot tell which.

\paragraph{C3, reliability.} The same battery, administered twice under an
identical protocol, returns the same verdict. A verdict that moves between
identical administrations is not a property of the model.

\paragraph{C4, threshold insensitivity.} The verdict survives a band of
thresholds rather than holding at one exact setting. A rule that produces its
answer only at the value chosen is a rule chosen for its answer.

\paragraph{C5, discriminant validity.} The verdict is not reproduced by a
cheaper signal: the model name, the vendor's size tier, reported parse health,
or a single question. This is the criterion the field is failing, it is the one
that cannot be satisfied by running the battery more carefully, and it is the
one the battery studied here does not meet.

\paragraph{C6, non-circularity.} The screen never reads the outcome variable. A
selection rule that can see the quantity the study reports can be tuned to it,
whatever order things were run in, and no statement of good faith substitutes
for the structural property.

C1 to C4 and C6 are satisfiable by careful protocol design. C5 is not: it is an
empirical question about the endpoints screened, and it can only be answered by
screening endpoints whose cheap signal and whose measured comprehension point
in opposite directions.

The instrument is a frozen bank of twenty probes over six domains, asked three
times each, giving sixty scored answers per endpoint.
Table~\ref{tab:bank} gives its structure. An endpoint is admitted only if it
answers at least 80\% of its sixty answers correctly, at least 75\% within
state reconstruction, and at least 75\% within terminal scoring. Expected
payoff is scored and recorded but gates nothing, and the artifact itself
carries that decision as a field rather than leaving it to the write-up.

Three properties make the nine endpoints comparable. All answered the same bank,
whose SHA-256 hash begins \texttt{2953fb47}, and read the same task description,
whose hash begins \texttt{b80981a5}; the scoring script fails closed and refuses
to tabulate an endpoint whose hashes differ. The bank hash is the same in all
three administrations reported in \S\ref{sec:res-stability}. And the screen
scores answers against ground truth without reading a single game, which is C6.

The endpoints are nine commercial routes from three vendors. We write
\emph{endpoint} rather than \emph{model} throughout, because what was measured
is a hosted route on a date and not a set of weights, and the Limitations
section returns to what that costs.

\label{sec:families}

State reconstruction on one state structure is a task, not a construct, so one
game cannot support a claim about comprehension screening. The test that
separates the two is transfer: if an endpoint's state-reconstruction score on
one game predicts its score on a game whose state has a different shape, the
battery measures something that travels. If it does not, a screen has to be
rebuilt for every environment, which is a finding a practitioner needs just as
much.

Three families would settle it, and this study has one of them. The family we
administered has a state made of two progress counters and an accumulated
private risk. The two that are missing would put the same six domains on a
state of a different kind: an iterated social dilemma, where what must be
tracked is the opponent's history, and a public-goods or common-pool game,
where what must be tracked is an aggregate over every player. Both would use
the same roster, the same three repetitions and the same scoring rule, with the
bank hash recorded, so that a per-endpoint per-domain score is comparable
across families.

\subsection{Comprehension fractionates}
\label{sec:res-fractionate}
% EVIDENCE  Per-domain accuracy over nine endpoints.
% ARTIFACT  results/frontier/admission_campaign_v6/**/admission.json
% STATUS    HAVE. Every number recomputed directly from the nine artifacts.
% FIGURE    An endpoints-by-domains accuracy matrix would carry this better
%           than the table and does not exist in this directory yet.

An aggregate screen score averages a domain that separates nothing with the
only domain that separates anything. Table~\ref{tab:fractionate} is the
evidence. Reading the stage payoff matrix is solved perfectly by all nine
endpoints, so that domain contributes exactly zero variance to the aggregate
while contributing a tenth of its weight. Recalling the stated rules is solved
perfectly by eight of the nine. At the other end, no endpoint reaches the gate
value on closed-form expected payoff and five score zero, so that domain
separates the endpoints hardly at all either, in the opposite direction.

What separates the endpoints is rebuilding the current state of a game in
progress, and it separates them across almost the entire range, from every
answer correct to one in five. The aggregate spans a much narrower band than
that, because the domains everybody solves pull it upward. A reader given only
the aggregate would conclude that the weakest endpoint understands half the
task; the per-domain result is that it reconstructs the state on one answer
in five while reciting the rules and the payoff matrix nearly perfectly.

This is C2, and it is the reason C2 belongs in the list. Reporting the
aggregate alone is not a summary of these nine rows. It is a different
measurement, and a less informative one.

\label{sec:res-format}

Parse health is not a screen, and on this campaign it is worse than useless: it
points the wrong way. Of the 540 probe answers, 535 could be read by the
semantic scorer. The five that could not are all in the expected-payoff domain,
the domain the battery already records as gating nothing, and all five are on
endpoints the battery admitted. In the gameplay the battery gates, the nine
endpoints produced 5{,}022 decisions across 270 games with no parse failure at
all, while their comprehension spanned the full range of
Table~\ref{tab:fractionate}. A parser satisfied everywhere cannot separate
anything.

A separate battery on a separate endpoint shows the dissociation at its
sharpest, and in the other direction. On a four-domain battery of 256 scored
answers, administered under sixteen presentations of the same game, recalling
the rules was answered correctly every time while the requested answer format
was produced not once; terminal scoring produced the requested format on
almost every answer and was correct on fewer than one in five.
Table~\ref{tab:format} gives both. A reader who screened on format would have
admitted the domain the endpoint could not do and refused the domain it could.

\subsection{Reliability and threshold sensitivity}
\label{sec:res-stability}
% EVIDENCE  Test-retest across three administrations; threshold sweep;
%           which condition binds; two admitted endpoints at margin zero.
% ARTIFACT  results/frontier/admission_campaign/ (128-token cap),
%           results/frontier/admission_campaign_v5/,
%           results/frontier/admission_campaign_v6/;

% STATUS    HAVE for all four. The sweep was re-run, not inherited.

The battery has been administered three times, and the same bank and task
description were used every time. Two administrations differ in nothing that is
recorded; the earliest differs from them only in the output token cap, 128
against 256. Table~\ref{tab:retest} gives the four endpoints present in all
three.

\paragraph{Between the two identical administrations the instrument moves and
the verdict does not.} Two of the four endpoints moved, each by two answers in
fifteen on state reconstruction, and the other two did not move at all. No
verdict changed. Across the three administrations together, one endpoint's
state-reconstruction score took three different values spanning 13.3 points,
which is wider than the 6.7-point band that decides that endpoint's membership.
That is C3 passed at the level of the verdict and failed at the level of the
score, and it is why a screen result is a decision rather than a trait
estimate.

\paragraph{One verdict has moved.} The endpoint at the bottom of the admitted
set was refused under the 128-token cap and admitted under 256. A truncated
answer is scored wrong and a low cap can truncate, so this is not a clean
repeat and we do not present it as one. It is reported because a screen verdict
is being used elsewhere as an entry condition, and a reader is entitled to know
that this endpoint's membership has a history.

\paragraph{The verdict survives a band of thresholds.} Each declared value
carries 5.0 points of headroom, and the three ranges hold together rather than
one at a time: the admitted set is identical at all 676 threshold combinations
inside them. Loosening is asymmetric. Two conditions can be taken to zero
without admitting anyone new, and the third is one achievable step from
changing the answer: taking state reconstruction from 75\% to 73.3\%, the next
score the fifteen-answer grid allows, admits one further endpoint and nobody
else. That is C4 passed, with the exception stated rather than averaged away.

\paragraph{Only one condition binds, and two admitted endpoints clear by
nothing.} State reconstruction alone at 75\% reproduces the admitted set
exactly; overall accuracy alone admits six endpoints and terminal scoring alone
admits seven. So two of the three gate conditions refuse nobody the first does
not, and most of the battery is idle on this sample. Worse for the instrument,
the margin on the condition that does bind can be zero. A fifteen-answer domain
at a 75\% threshold asks for 11.25 correct answers, so the lowest score that
clears is 12 of 15. One admitted endpoint clears state reconstruction at
exactly that score and another clears terminal scoring at exactly that score.
One answer otherwise would put the first at 73.3\%, which is the score on which
a refused endpoint is refused. A pass margin can therefore be narrower than the
movement the same instrument shows between two identical administrations.

A sixty-call battery has to be worth sixty calls, and on this roster it is not.
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} puts four cheaper signals against the same nine
verdicts. Three of them reproduce it exactly, and the cheapest of those costs
nothing at all.

\paragraph{The endpoint name.} Calling an endpoint small when \texttt{mini},
\texttt{nano} or \texttt{lite} appears as a whole word of its route string
splits these nine into exactly the two sets the battery produced. All four
refused endpoints carry such a word and none of the five admitted ones does.
Enumerating all $C(9,4)=126$ ways of relabelling which four names carry a size
word gives an exact one-sided permutation probability of $1/126$, or $0.008$.
The signal costs nothing and is available before a single call is made.

\paragraph{One probe.} Two of the twenty probes reproduce the verdict on their
own at three calls each, a twentieth of the battery. Searching twenty probes
for the best one is a multiple comparison and we charge it: enumerating all 126
possible five-of-nine labellings, some probe in the bank reaches perfect
agreement on only two of them, so the exact probability of a match this good
under an arbitrary labelling, with the search over twenty probes included, is
$2/126$, or $0.016$.

\paragraph{One domain.} Only state reconstruction reproduces the verdict as a
domain, which is the same result as \S\ref{sec:res-stability} reached from the
threshold side. The other five domains reach at best eight of nine.

\paragraph{Parse health.} Reported parse health does no better than admitting
everybody, five of nine, and its errors fall on admitted endpoints.

None of this shows that the battery measures nothing. Comprehension that simply
tracked vendor size tier across these nine endpoints would produce exactly this
picture, and nine endpoints cannot tell the two apart. What follows is narrower
and is still fatal to the instrument's cost case: on this roster the battery
draws no line that the endpoint name already draws, and no claim resting on the
admitted-against-refused split is separable from a claim about model scale.
That is C5, unmet. A screen meets it the moment one endpoint breaks the tie: a
large-tier endpoint that fails state reconstruction, or a small-tier endpoint
that passes it. This roster contains none, and finding one is a collection
problem rather than an analysis problem.

The battery's score is associated with the behaviour it gates, and the
association is not independent evidence that the battery works.
Table~\ref{tab:downstream} reports it. All nine endpoints also played the same
game under one frozen protocol, thirty games and 558 decisions each, and each

probability is small. Against the aggregate battery score instead of the

highest-scoring refused endpoint shifts its play almost exactly as far as the
lowest admitted one, 37.6 points against 38.2, so the two sets touch rather
than separate even though their means are far apart.

\subsection{What the screen catches, and what it misses}
\label{sec:res-seat}
% EVIDENCE  Two symmetric seats in the same game are interchangeable by

% STATUS    HAVE, BOTH SIDES. The per-endpoint opening rows exist under the

A screen should catch an endpoint that fails an invariance the task guarantees,
and this one half does. The game seats two players symmetrically: at the
opening move the two prompts are identical byte for byte under a swap of the
two seat names, and the regenerating script checks that on all 270 openings
rather than assuming it. An endpoint that plays the two seats differently is
responding to an ordinal label rather than to the state.

Over a whole game the screen separates the endpoints cleanly.
Table~\ref{tab:seat} gives it. One refused endpoint plays its two seats
19.4 points apart; every other endpoint is within 2.2 points of symmetric; the
pooled gap over all nine is small and excludes zero only because of that one
endpoint, and removing it leaves an interval that straddles zero. Over the five
admitted endpoints together the gap also straddles zero. That is the strongest
convergent evidence in this paper that the screen selects on something real,
and it is stronger than \S\ref{sec:res-downstream} because it is a
within-endpoint contrast against a known invariance rather than a correlation
over nine points.

At the opening move, which is the one place where the contrast is a pure label
effect, the separation disappears. The refused endpoint opens 30.0 points
apart, and an admitted endpoint opens further apart than that, 33.3 points,
having never once opened unsafely from the first seat. A second admitted
endpoint is at 16.7 points. Four of the nine open the same way from both seats
in every game and can show no gap at all. So the honest statement is narrower
than it first looks: the screen separates endpoints on a seat asymmetry that
persists through a game, and does not separate them on the cleanest measurement
of the same failure. We report both because the second is the one a reader
would otherwise discover.

\subsection{A screen refuses a whole measurement set}
\label{sec:res-refusal}
% EVIDENCE  What a screen costs when it works. On a different endpoint and a
%           four-domain battery, the screen refused, and a completed campaign
%           of 768 games, 13,680 decisions and 1,536 fixed-state replay rows
%           was demoted to diagnostic rather than reported as behaviour.
%           On that endpoint, swapping which of two opaque response codes
%           means safe moves play by 24.0 to 57.3 points at an identical
%           saved state, across all eight retellings, every interval
%           excluding zero. The screen's own aggregate barely moves under the
%           same sixteen presentations, 50.0 to 62.5 per cent.
% ARTIFACT  results/open_source/context_skin_pilot/analysis_live_pilot_t0/
%             analysis_summary.json (admission_passed false, claim_status),
%             context_mapping_diagnostic.csv, comprehension_by_cell.csv,
%             quality_coverage.csv
% STATUS    HAVE.

A screen is only worth its cost if refusing changes what gets reported, and
here it did. On a separate endpoint, screened by a four-domain battery, the
screen refused on state update and terminal scoring, and a completed campaign
of 768 games, 13{,}680 decisions and 1{,}536 fixed-state replays was recorded
as diagnostic rather than read as behaviour. Nothing was wrong with the
collection: every shard completed, coverage was rectangular and no response
failed to parse.

What that campaign shows is why the refusal was right. Replaying the same saved
states while swapping which of two opaque response codes means safe moves the
endpoint's play by between 24.0 and 57.3 percentage points, in the same
direction under all eight retellings of the game, every interval excluding
zero. A relabelling that a correct model of the game must ignore rewrites the
action sequence. An analysis that had read those trajectories as policy would
have been reading the response surface.

The screen itself barely noticed. Across all sixteen presentations its
aggregate accuracy moved only between 50.0 and 62.5 per cent, a range of two
answers in sixteen. So the instrument is stable under re-presentation while the
behaviour it gates is not, which is the property a screen needs, and it is the
one piece of evidence here that this kind of battery earns anything at all.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% Write last. The closing argument is the framework in sec:construct with C5
% as the operative criterion, not a summary of the nine-endpoint table.
% =====================================================================

A comprehension screen decides what a behavioural study is about, so it should
be held to what any measuring instrument is held to. We set out six criteria
and applied them to one battery. It passes reliability at the level of the
verdict, passes threshold insensitivity with one stated exception, and reports
per-domain scores that show its aggregate is carried by a single subtask while
another subtask contributes no variance at all. It fails the criterion that
matters most. On this roster its sixty scored answers draw a line that three
answers draw, and that no answers draw: a regular expression over the endpoint
name reproduces the verdict exactly.

That failure is not an argument against screening. It is an argument for
reporting what a screen buys, in the same table as the screen. The cheap
baselines cost nothing to compute, they are computable from data any screening
study already has, and a study that reports them either earns its battery or
learns that it could have read the model names. We recommend that any study
gating agents on comprehension report the per-domain scores, the threshold band,
and the comparison against the name, one probe and parse health, and that it
say which of its gate conditions refused anybody.

\section*{Limitations}
% ARR requires an unnumbered Limitations section; it does not count against
% the page limit. Write it, do not pad it, and put the real ones in it.
% ARTIFACT  L1: results/frontier/baseline_campaign_v6/**/run_manifest.json
%           carries no language field and one fixed prompt version.
% STATUS    HAVE.
% =====================================================================

Nine endpoints, one game, one prompt version. No language field is recorded
anywhere in the collection, so no cross-lingual claim is available and none is
made. Every route-level statistic here rests on $n=9$; the permutation tests are
exact, which bounds the false-positive rate but says nothing about generality.

The screen's verdict is confounded with vendor size tier by construction on this
sample, and every association with downstream behaviour inherits that confound.
This is the paper's own central result and it is also its largest limitation:
we can show that the battery fails the discriminant-validity criterion here, and
we cannot show what it would do on a roster that breaks the tie, because no such
roster has been screened.

Every result is from one battery on one state structure, so the paper measures a
task and not a construct. The transfer evidence that would change that is named
in \S\ref{sec:families} and has not been collected. Until it is, the criteria of
\S\ref{sec:construct} are a standard argued for rather than a standard
demonstrated across environments.

The endpoints are commercial routes, not models. They can change under a fixed
name, and one verdict in this study already moved when a decoding setting
changed. A screen verdict is a statement about a route on a date, and treating
it as a property of a model is a category error this paper is partly about.

The two comparisons drawn from separate instruments, in
\S\ref{sec:res-format} and \S\ref{sec:res-refusal}, are read only within their
own instrument. Their scores are never pooled with the nine-endpoint battery,
and the agreement between the fractionation patterns is a description rather
than a replication.

The twenty probes are, by domain:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Rule recall}: the hidden horizon, the minimum number of rounds,
      simultaneity of the two moves, the stopping probability.
\item \emph{Stage payoff}: the two off-diagonal cells of the stage matrix.
\item \emph{State reconstruction}: own progress, opponent progress, the gap
      between them, own accumulated risk, own accumulated payoff.
\item \emph{State transition}: progress after one move, accumulated risk after
      one move.
\item \emph{Terminal scoring}: who wins, the winner's payoff, the winner's
      accumulated risk, the loser's setback, the payoff after a setback.
\item \emph{Expected payoff}: the closed form under two action profiles.
\end{itemize}

Each is asked three times at a requested temperature of zero, scored against
ground truth by a semantic scorer that reads a scalar or a listed option rather
than requiring an exact output format. That choice is deliberate and it is what
makes \S\ref{sec:res-format} measurable: format compliance is recorded
separately from correctness instead of being folded into it.
